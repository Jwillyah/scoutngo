.DISCUSSION...
As of 230 PM EST Monday...

KEY MESSAGE 1...Dry weather continues into the middle of this week 
with above average temperatures.

High pressure gradually shifts offshore Tuesday as upper-level 
ridging builds across the eastern US. This will result in dry 
conditions and a warming trend through midweek. Mostly sunny skies 
are expected for Tuesday with highs ranging from the lower to mid 
70s. Temperatures warm further Wednesday as low level flow becomes 
takes on a southerly component, with highs reaching the mid to upper 
70s across much of the area. A few locations across interior 
southern VA and northeast NC could approach 80 degrees. Temperatures 
will likely be somewhat cooler along the immediate coastline due to 
an onshore wind. 

Skies gradually clear out this evening into tonight, though some 
patchy fog cannot be ruled out as we approach sunrise Tuesday AM. 
Temperatures drop back into the 40s for most areas tonight. Mostly 
clear skies and light winds are expected Tuesday night with lows 
generally in the 40s to lower 50s. Increasing moisture and clouds 
ahead of the next system should result in a milder Wednesday night, 
with lows mainly in the 50s to around 60.


KEY MESSAGE 2...Rain chances increase Thursday into Friday, with the 
greatest chances likely Friday. Locally heavy rainfall is possible, 
although uncertainty remains regarding rainfall amounts and 
placement.

The primary forecast challenge for later this week continues to 
revolve around the timing of an approaching upper trough and 
associated cold front. Latest 12z deterministic guidance has come 
into slightly better agreement, with both the GFS and ECMWF starting 
to favor Friday as the primary period for precipitation. Of note, 
the GFS has now trended a bit slower with the upper trough compared 
to previous runs. 

While some showers still look possible on Thursday into Thursday 
night, the more widespread precipitation appears increasingly likely 
to occur Friday as the front slowly progresses across the area. Both 
the 12z GFS and ECMWF depict a more substantial rainfall signal 
Friday, particularly across eastern and central portions of the 
area. The ECMWF even depicts localized QPF maxima exceeding 2 
inches. However, considerable uncertainties remains regarding the 
exact placement and magnitude of the heavier precipitation, 
especially given the potentially convective nature of the rainfall. 

Although confidence is increasing Friday being the primary 
precipitation period, ensemble guidance has been less aggressive 
with rainfall amounts compared to some of the deterministic 
solutions. Thus, would be hesitant to place too much emphasis on 
the localized QPF maximum at this time. Still, the potentially for 
locally heavy rainfall will at least need to monitored, particularly 
for E/SE portions of the area. In addition, a few thunderstorms will 
be possible Thursday night into Friday, although the degree of 
instability remains uncertain. 

Temperatures will remain above normal ahead of the front, with only 
modest cooling expected behind it. Generally drier conditions are 
expected for the weekend, although some uncertainty remains 
regarding the progression of the front/potential lingering rain 
along the immediate coast.

As of 635 PM EST Monday...

VFR conditions should prevail for the next few hours. Later

fog and low CIGs as winds turn calm and mid-level clouds depart.
The fog could be dense at times and have introduced mention of 
1/2 SM VSBYs to all terminals with this update. Flight 
restrictions could last until ~14z, before skies quickly clear 
by the late morning and afternoon. VFR is then expected for the 
rest of the day with light E winds at ORF, PHF, and ECG and 
light S/SSE winds at RIC and SBY. 

Outlook: Dry weather and mostly VFR conditions are expected through
at least Wednesday. However, there is some potential for
additional fog Tuesday night/early Wednesday. The next chance 
for rain comes Thursday into Friday, but confidence remains low 
on coverage and any potential restrictions.

.MARINE...
As of 230 PM EST Monday...

Key Messages:

- Sub-SCA conditions prevails through mid-week before a potential 
weather system approaches. 

Afternoon weather analysis shows broad high pressure centered over 
the area. Winds are currently light ~10kt out of the NE and seas are

10kt with an occasional gust upwards of 15kt. By Wednesday, high

to remain sub-SCA as SST have cooled off and less mixing is likely 
to occur. The main threat for any SCA will likely come from wave 
heights, as sells approach from the SE. However, confidence is low 
due to the nature of the coastline likely protecting the area from 
the swells and the direction of the wind.

Updated aviation forecast for the 18z TAFs.

&&

.KEY MESSAGES...
1) Dry weather continues into the middle of this week with a gradual 
warming trend.

2) A chance for showers returns Thursday and Friday, but confidence 
in widespread beneficial rainfall remains low, though most areas 
likely see at least 0.10" of rain. Above average temperatures are 
also expected to continue through at least the end of the week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...
As of 225 AM EST Monday...

KEY MESSAGE 1...Dry weather continues into the middle of this week 
with a gradual warming trend. 

A well-defined area of high pressure is still just off the NJ coast 
early this morning, with sfc ridging extending SSW into the
local area. As a result, there is a very weak pressure gradient
and light/calm winds at this hour. A weak shortwave aloft over 
the central Appalachians continues to slowly move toward the 
local area. High clouds have overspread much of VA/NC in advance
of this feature. Temperatures are in the upper 30s-40s and will
not fall much more overnight due to the cloud cover. Radar 
mosaic shows some light echoes over SE WV and SW VA, with some 
sites in SW VA reporting light rain at this hour. Clouds 
increase today as that shortwave weakens as it approaches and 
crosses the area. Highs will be only in the mid to upper 60s due
to the clouds. There is a very low but nonzero chance of light 
rain in the Piedmont later this morning- midday but rain amounts
would only be a few hundredths of an inch at best. Temps may 
struggle to get out of the lower 60s across western portions of 
the FA if we manage to see some very light rain. 

A pronounced warming trend takes place on Tuesday/Wednesday as a 
building ridge aloft moves over the area while the surface high 
moves farther offshore. With the low-level flow becoming SE, lower 
to mid 70s are expected on Tuesday with mid to upper 70s on 
Wednesday.  Depending on cloud cover, 80F readings are across 
portions of interior srn VA and NE NC Wednesday. Overnight lows also 
gradually moderate from the 40s tonight to around 60F Wednesday 
night. 


KEY MESSAGE 2...A chance for showers returns Thursday and Friday, 
but confidence in widespread beneficial rainfall remains low, though 
most areas likely see at least 0.10" of rain. Above average 
temperatures are also expected to continue through at least the 
end of the week.

A strong low pressure system at the surface and aloft will initially 
move from the Rockies into the northern Plains and Midwest early in 
the week, while a trailing cold front eventually tries to advance 
toward the area in the Thursday-Friday timeframe. Shower chances 
return on Thursday as the trailing cold front gets close to the 
area. Showers could begin by late morning west of I-95, with 
precipitation chances expanding to cover the entire area by the end 
of the day. However, most of the models show a weakening trend in 
the showers as they approach the area as the best upper forcing 
misses us to the north. Despite this, most areas will likely see at 
least 0.10" of rain from Thursday-Thursday night. Shower chances 
likely end across the Piedmont by Friday morning following the FROPA 
but continue during the day/evening on Friday across the eastern 2/3 
of the FA (especially near the coast) as some redevelopment is 
possible before the weak cold front crosses the rest of the area. 
Again, expect more in the way of scattered showers versus widespread 
rain from Thu-Fri. Most areas will see measurable rain but ensemble 
probabilities for >0.5" are only 10-30% on the EPS and 30-60% on the 
GEFS/CMCE. Highs remain at least in the low-mid 70s through the end 
of the week, with dew points potentially rising well into the 60s by 
late week. Mainly dry weather likely returns on Saturday. Another 
shortwave may move across the area late Sunday-Monday, but this 
system will likely be moisture starved as the deeper moisture likely 
gets shunted offshore after Friday's FROPA.

There won't be much in the way of appreciable CAA behind the front, 
and above average temperatures may continue through next weekend. 
There remain no potential freezes in the forecast as the more 
substantial cold air remains in the northern half of Canada for the 
foreseeable future.
